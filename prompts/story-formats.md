# Story formats for short H3 videos

A gist is short: usually a joke, a twist, or one small moment. The FORMAT decides how it must be staged to work on video. Pick the format by how the payoff works, not by the topic.

## Formats

### dialogue_reversal
A harmless setup line gets a reply, then a twist line reframes everything ("I'll always help you in tough times." / "I'm not going through anything." / "I'm not talking about now.").
- Payoff: the twist line, then the listener's face realising it.
- Beats: setup line → reply that sets the trap → short silent beat (0.5–1 s) → twist line in close-up → held silent reaction (2 s or more). The reaction is its own beat and the last one.
- Delivery: on-screen dialogue only. No narrator.

### logic_twist
A rule or observation is stated, then a counter-intuitive rule, then an explanation that makes the audience re-read the first part (riddles, "A does X, but B does Y because…", wordplay reasoning).
- Payoff: the moment the reasoning clicks for the viewer.
- Beats: one beat that VISUALLY PROVES each rule (show the situation, do not just talk about it) → the reveal beat (the detail that flips it, e.g. a dry *click*, a look, a gesture) as its own beat with a clear sound or visual cue → a reaction beat that shows the flipped situation.
- Delivery: the rules are spoken by an off-screen NARRATOR (voiceover) over the scenes that prove them; characters mostly react. Keep each narrated sentence short and verbatim from the gist.
- The logic must be literally true in what is shown. Counts, who is scared/happy/fooled, before/after states must match the words exactly.

### visual_gag
The payoff is something seen, not said (slapstick, a reveal of what was off-screen, a prop doing something unexpected).
- Beats: establish the normal situation → build expectation → the gag in one clear, well-framed beat → reaction.
- Delivery: little or no dialogue; sound effects matter.

### misunderstanding
One character interprets words or a situation differently from the audience or another character (dramatic irony).
- Beats: establish what the audience knows → the misreading line or action → consequence → reaction or realisation.
- Delivery: on-screen dialogue; make the true meaning visible to the audience in the frame.

### escalation
Rule of three or a growing pattern where the last step breaks it.
- Beats: first instance → second instance (same framing, slightly bigger) → third instance breaks the pattern → reaction.
- Delivery: repeat framing and timing exactly so the break is obvious.

### callback
An early detail comes back as the payoff.
- Beats: plant the detail clearly (close-up or a line) → unrelated middle → the detail returns → reaction.

### narrative
A non-comedy story beat or scene (drama, romance, action, slice of life).
- Beats: establish → development → turn → resolution or emotional hold.

### emotional_moment
One feeling carried by performance (a confession, a goodbye, a surprise gift).
- Beats: before state → trigger → the emotion growing in close-up → quiet hold.

## Comedy timing (all comedy formats)

- One idea per beat. Never stack the setup and the punchline in the same beat.
- Silence before the punchline: 0.5–1 s with nobody speaking.
- The punchline is delivered in a close-up or medium close-up of whoever says it or whoever it lands on.
- The video ends on a held reaction (2 s or more, silent), not on the last spoken word.
- Reactions are physical and specific (eyebrows, eyes, mouth, freezing, a slow blink), never "reacts".
- Laughter words inside dialogue ("Ha ha") do not mean the character is happy; state the real emotion.
- Keep the tone light: exaggerated but grounded acting, warm or neutral light, no dark thriller mood unless the gist asks for it.

## Sensitive props and situations

- Weapons, crime, danger: keep it an obvious comedy. No one is hurt, nothing is fired at a person, no blood; fear is played with exaggerated comic reactions. Keep the prop the gist needs (a real-looking gun if the joke depends on it being real) but never point it at the camera.
- No real people, celebrities or trademarked characters.
